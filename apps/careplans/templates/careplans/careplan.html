Nursing Interventions & Scientific Rationales
InterventionScientific Rationale
Assess patient's baseline knowledge, health literacy (How do you prefer to get health information?), preferred language, sensory limitations, and identified barriers to learning (anxiety, pain, cultural factors); document readiness to learnLearning assessment identifies individualized barriers that must be addressed before education begins. Adequate pain control and anxiety reduction are prerequisites for effective learning. Cultural competence improves engagement and retention
Use teach-back method for all education: provide information in small chunks, then ask "In your own words what did you do when..." to document patient understanding verba...Teach-back returns demonstration of understanding is the gold-standard method for confirming health literacy. Studies show up to 90% of patients don't understand discharge instructions when standard approaches are used; teach-back reduces 30-day readmissions
Provide written educational materials at 4-6th grade reading level in patient's preferred language; use visual aids, videos, or models as appropriate; avoid medical jargonNearly 50% of US adults have limited health literacy. Low health literacy is associated with increased hospitalization, poor medication adherence, and worse outcomes. Plain-language supporting materials improve retention by 60%
Involve family members or identified caregivers in education sessions (with patient consent); provide time for written materials; address caregiver-specific concerns and responsibilitiesFamily/caregiver involvement is critical for post-discharge adherence, especially for complex regimens. Support systems reduce caregiver burden and prevent rehospitalization related to missed care
Begin discharge planning on day 1 in collaboration with interdisciplinary team (case management, social work, pharmacy, PT/OT, wound care); document referrals for safe discharge and follow-up appointmentsEarly discharge planning identifies and resolves barriers proactively. Multidisciplinary discharge planning reduces 30-day readmission rates by 25% (JCAHO, CMS). Late referrals are the leading cause of avoidable prolonged hospitalizations
