Academic Foundation

Decades of landmark research reach the same conclusion: asset allocation is the dominant driver of long-term portfolio outcomes.

Brinson, Hood & Beebower — and the research that followed

Brinson, Hood & Beebower · 1986
Determinants of Portfolio Performance

Studied 91 large US pension funds over a decade. Strategic asset allocation explained 93.6% of quarterly return variation — dwarfing security selection and market timing combined.

Brinson, Singer & Beebower · 1991
Determinants of Portfolio Performance II

Follow-up study of 82 funds confirmed the original findings. Asset allocation dominates. Active bets add noise, not value. Getting the allocation right matters more than picking the manager.

The ETF Approach

Control what you can.
ETFs are the smarter instrument.

Rather than searching for the rare manager who might outperform — and paying heavily for the search — Wilbury Capital uses low-cost ETFs to capture market returns efficiently and direct the fee savings back to clients.

Capture Market Returns Efficiently

ETFs deliver broad index exposure with minimal tracking error. Clients receive the return the market offers without dilution from active decisions that, on average, subtract value.

Lower Fees = Higher Net Returns

Average ETF expense ratio: 0.05–0.20%. Average active fund: 0.75–1.25%. Over 20 years, a 1% fee difference on $5M compounds to over $1.1M in foregone client wealth.

Full Transparency & Daily Liquidity

Daily holdings disclosure, intraday liquidity, no lock-up periods. Clients always know exactly what they own and can act immediately — critical for institutional-grade governance.

Eliminate Manager Selection Risk

No need to research, monitor, or replace underperforming managers. The structural alpha from disciplined allocation and cost savings replaces the illusion of manager alpha.
